The international football network

Treat every nation as a node and every match as an edge. The shape of 150 years of results explains why ranking national teams — and seeding a 48-team World Cup — is genuinely hard: the world barely plays itself.

1 · Teams almost never leave home

86% of games are played within a team's own confederation. Only about one match in seven crosses continents — so the evidence linking, say, an Asian side to a South American one is desperately thin.

2 · The continents draw themselves

An algorithm knowing nothing of geography recovers the confederations from match patterns alone (NMI 0.96) — but finds five blocs, not six: CONMEBOL and CONCACAF fuse into one Americas super-bloc, they play each other so much.

3 · A ranking held up by a few bridges

PageRank on the beat-graph gives a power ranking for free. The whole global ordering leans on a handful of bridge teams — — who actually venture across continents.
